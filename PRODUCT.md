# Product

## Register

product

## Users

MoMo is for individuals and households who want to track everyday spending without turning personal finance into a spreadsheet habit. Users are often logging expenses in the moment, comparing personal and shared household spend, or checking stats only when they need a clear read on where money went.

## Product Purpose

MoMo makes expense tracking feel as easy as texting. The core workflow is chat-first: type a natural expense message, let MoMo parse and categorize it, then use lightweight stats to understand personal or household spending. Success means fast capture, clear categorization, reliable shared updates, and useful analytics without finance-app heaviness.

## Brand Personality

Fun, easy, and a bit edgy. MoMo should feel direct and playful without becoming childish, judgmental, or gimmicky. The voice can be cheeky when the stakes are low, but it should stay clear and helpful around money, errors, privacy, and household collaboration.

## Anti-references

MoMo should not feel like corporate fintech, spreadsheet-first budgeting software, sterile SaaS dashboards, gamified savings guilt, or an overly polished AI finance coach. Avoid visual and copy patterns that make money feel like a compliance task, a moral scorecard, or a generic analytics product.

## Design Principles

- Chat is the product surface. Prefer interactions that preserve the feeling of writing a message over form-heavy expense entry.
- Make money feel less boring. Keep the interface lively, colorful, and tactile while still making totals, statuses, and actions easy to understand.
- Be useful without nagging. Stats and corrections should appear when needed, not interrupt the core logging flow.
- Respect household context. Shared spending should feel collaborative and clear, with privacy boundaries and invitations handled plainly.
- Keep confidence visible. Parsing, categorization, sync state, and error recovery should make it obvious what happened and what the user can do next.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Aim for accessible contrast, keyboard-reachable controls, screen-reader friendly labels, and reduced-motion alternatives for animated or scroll-driven surfaces. Avoid relying on color alone for financial status, message processing state, or chart interpretation.
